1. What this policy covers

This policy describes how the Windows app, its optional AI setup assistant, and the public download website handle information. It is written for the current design: a local desktop viewer with optional help from a model that runs on a private server, not a general cloud camera service.

If we later host Ara on a public cloud, invite customer sites onto our private network, or add online accounts, we will update this policy before that change goes live.

2. Factual assumptions (read this first)

The private GitHub repo LeaveUmWet/Reolink currently holds design documents, not a complete application with lockfiles we could audit. The points below are taken from those docs and from /workspace/reolink-viewer/project-plan.md. Anything marked assumption must be confirmed against the shipping build.

  1. Camera credentials are stored on the user’s Windows PC, in profile data under %APPDATA%/Reolink/ (or a similar local path), encrypted with Windows DPAPI. That is what docs/architecture.md and docs/setup-wizard.md specify. This is not Windows Credential Manager. (An earlier assumption that Credential Manager would be used came from a different project and is not what the viewer docs say.)
  2. A master password and optional super-admin and user-role passwords are hashed locally (bcrypt or argon2). Viewing may be allowed while settings are locked; editing requires the master password.
  3. Live RTSP (and similar) streams are pulled and viewed locally. A local Node process may proxy or transcode a stream (for example RTSP to HLS) on localhost. Stream video and audio are not designed to be uploaded to us.
  4. Snapshots, time-lapse frames, and optional recordings are saved only to folders the user chooses (defaults discussed in the phase-1 prompt include Documents folders). They are not uploaded by the current design.
  5. Diagnostic logs are written locally to %APPDATA%/Reolink/logs/ with daily rotation. The phase-1 prompt requires that logs never contain raw passwords.
  6. Ara does not receive camera passwords, tokens, API keys, or RTSP URLs that embed credentials. If a user types a secret into the chat box, the app should strip or refuse it and point the user to the credential fields.
  7. When Ara is used, the app sends the question text and retrieved setup-document chunks to the AI host over a private mesh (Tailscale or ZeroTier). The committed default host is the operator’s machine called G-Tech (also called K17), running Ollama and Llama 3.2. An optional backup host (PelusoDesktop) may be used only if enabled and idle.
  8. Voice audio may be processed on the user’s PC or on G-Tech. The project plan leaves this open. Until the shipping path is fixed, this policy describes both possibilities.
  9. Tonight’s architecture is for the operator’s own machines and invited networks. The project plan itself says a customer PC will not reach G-Tech unless that site is invited onto the mesh. Shipping “just talk to G-Tech” to paying customers without a new design would send customer questions to the vendor. That is not enabled as a silent default in this draft, and it needs a separate privacy notice and a lawyer’s review before anyone does it.
  10. A license-key check is planned (not confirmed as built): the app may send a license key and a hashed machine fingerprint (described in docs/licensing.md as a hash of CPU ID and disk serial) to a licensing server, and may cache a signed token locally, DPAPI-encrypted. Auto-update checks of GitHub Releases are also planned.

If a shipping build differs from these assumptions, update this policy before you ship it.

3. Information the app stores on your computer

The following typically stay on the PC where the app is installed, unless you export or copy them:

  • Camera, NVR, and account usernames and passwords, and related connection settings (host, port, channel, display names), in encrypted profile files.
  • Layout and preference data (tile positions, theme, text size, kiosk and start-on-boot choices).
  • Local user accounts for the app (role-based logins), stored as password hashes.
  • License token or trial state, if licensing is enabled.
  • Logs of app events (startup, connection success or failure, reconnects, and similar).
  • Optional snapshots, time-lapse images, and recordings, in the folders you choose.
  • If Ara’s retrieval index is built on the PC, a local copy of setup-document embeddings may be stored there.

We do not receive a live copy of that local data in the current design, except as described in the sections on Ara, licensing, and the download website.

If you share or email a profile file, you may also share the credentials inside it. Treat exported profiles as sensitive.

4. Camera streams, footage, and audio

Live video and audio from your cameras are displayed on your computer. In the current design they are not uploaded to our servers.

If you turn on local recording, scheduled recording, event-triggered recording, snapshots, time-lapse, talk-back (two-way audio), or on-screen overlays, those features run on your computer and your network. You are responsible for complying with recording-consent, workplace-notice, and surveillance laws that apply to your site (see the Terms of Use). The app does not obtain consent from people who appear in or are heard on your cameras.

5. What Ara transmits

When you use Ara:

  • Sent to the AI host (G-Tech, or an optional backup you have enabled): the text of your question and the retrieved chunks of setup documentation used as context. Tool results, if any, are intended to be limited to status such as “stream OK / failed / codec,” not full URLs with passwords.
  • Not sent to the model, under the project rule: camera passwords, tokens, API keys, and RTSP URLs that contain credentials.
  • Voice: if you choose a voice conversation, your microphone audio is converted to text and Ara’s reply may be read aloud. That speech processing may happen on your PC, or the audio may be sent over the private mesh to G-Tech, depending on the shipping configuration. We will document the actual path in the About screen when it is fixed. Push-to-talk is the intended mode, not an always-on microphone.
  • Not used to train a public foundation model in the current design. Questions and chunks go to a private Ollama host for the purpose of answering you. If we later change hosts or training practices, we will update this policy.

If Ara is offline, the app should fall back to static help and the add-camera wizard and should not keep retrying in a way that leaks questions to an unexpected endpoint.

Customer deployments. Until we offer a customer-hosted model, a documented relay with a contract, or a cloud host with an updated policy, do not configure paying customers’ copies of the app to send questions to our G-Tech server. That would be a different data-processing arrangement.

6. Licensing, updates, and the download website

If license checks are enabled, we (or a provider who hosts the check for us) may receive:

  • the license key you enter,
  • a hashed machine fingerprint,
  • the result of the validation request (success, failure, or similar), and
  • standard server logs (IP address, time, user agent).

If auto-update is enabled, the app may contact GitHub to see whether a new installer is available.

The public landing page and installer are expected to be hosted on GitHub Pages and GitHub Releases (and possibly Cloudflare Pages). Those services may collect ordinary web logs and security data (for example IP address, browser type, and the file requested) under their own policies:

  • GitHub Privacy Statement
  • Cloudflare Privacy Policy (only if Cloudflare is used)

Downloading the installer does not require creating an account on our site. If we later sell licenses through a checkout provider (for example Stripe or Gumroad), that provider’s privacy policy will apply to payment data; we do not intend to receive full payment-card numbers.

8. Retention

  • On your PC: profiles, footage, and logs remain until you delete them, uninstall the app, or a log-rotation rule removes old log files. Uninstall may not delete recordings you saved under Documents or another folder you chose.
  • Ara on G-Tech: operators should configure the host so prompts are not kept longer than needed to produce an answer and to debug failures. A specific retention period is not yet implemented in the docs we reviewed; treat “retain only as long as needed for the session and short-term troubleshooting, then delete” as the intended rule, and document the actual period before paid sales.
  • Website and license logs: keep only as long as needed for security, abuse prevention, license enforcement, and legal obligations, then delete or anonymize. A numeric period (for example 12 months for ordinary web logs) should be filled in here before publication: [retention period].

9. Children

The app and website are not directed at children under 13 (or under 16 in jurisdictions that use that age). We do not knowingly collect personal information from children. If you believe a child has provided personal information to us, contact us and we will delete it.

10. Your choices

  • You can use the viewer without Ara; the add-camera wizard is intended to work with the assistant off.
  • You can choose typing instead of voice, and you can decline microphone access.
  • You can delete local profiles, logs, and saved media from your PC.
  • You can stop using the download site at any time.
  • If a CCPA or similar request right later applies to you, contact us at the email above. At first-customer scale we may not be a “business” under the CCPA; we will still try to help with access and deletion requests for information we actually hold (for example a license record).

11. Security

We design the app so secrets stay on your computer and are encrypted at rest with DPAPI, and so Ara is not given passwords. No method of transmission or storage is completely secure. You are responsible for the physical and network security of the PC, the cameras, and any mesh or VPN you join.

12. International users

We are based in Florida, United States. If you use the download site from another country, website logs may be processed in the United States by us or by GitHub/Cloudflare. We do not currently offer the product as an EU/EEA service. If that changes, we will add the required international-transfer and GDPR wording, and a lawyer should review it, before the offer is made.

13. Changes

If we make a material change (for example, sending Ara data to a cloud provider, or receiving customer camera credentials), we will provide a more prominent notice, and we will not start that practice on already-installed copies without a software update and notice.
